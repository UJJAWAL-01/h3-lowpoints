    About this map

    Where rain water collects

    Rain that cannot flow away gathers in low ground: a hollow in a field, a dip in a street, a basin between buildings. Elevation models call these closed depressions. This map shows every one on land in the lower 48 states, found by the open-source h3-lowpoints library on the USGS 3DEP elevation model, and drawn on H3 hexagons.

    How to read it

    • Across the country and at city scale, each hexagon says how much of its area lies in depressions.
    • At street scale (the finest level), each hexagon is shaded by how deep water stands there when its depression is full.
    • Click a hexagon to see its depression: how much it holds, the rain that fills it, how often that rain falls, and where it overflows.

    What it does not include

    • Storm drains, sewers, pipes and pumps. In cities they carry much of the water away.
    • Features smaller than the 30 m elevation data can see, such as most street-scale dips.
    • Rivers in flood, tides, snowmelt and frozen ground.

    It is not a flood map or a warning. In an emergency, follow local officials. How well these depressions match real flood reports, including what did not work, is in the evidence.
